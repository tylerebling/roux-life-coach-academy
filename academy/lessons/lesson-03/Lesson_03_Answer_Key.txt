LESSON 3 · ANSWER KEY

1. B · Meaning organized around an event
A story is the interpretation or conclusion attached to an event; it may be accurate, incomplete, or inaccurate.

2. C · My manager did not reply to two emails this week
A neutral observer or email record could confirm the lack of replies.

3. B · Trigger, story, response, result
The map tracks the event, meaning, emotional/behavioral response, and resulting outcome.

4. C · To understand the safety or relief it tries to create
Understanding function lowers shame and supports more informed choice.

5. B · What happened, and what did you make it mean?
The question distinguishes observable events from interpretation without shaming.

6. B · The feeling and need can make sense even when the story is unproven
Validation honors experience without certifying an interpretation.

7. C · Fit the evidence and restore agency
A strong reframe is complete, believable, evidence-based, and client-owned.

8. B · It is small, responsible, client-owned, and gathers information
Experiments should create useful evidence without coercion or overwhelm.

9. B · Separate the behavior from the identity verdict
Specific behavior can be understood and repaired; global identity shame blocks learning.

10. C · When severe distress, danger, or impairment requires licensed care
Serious safety or clinical needs require appropriate professional support.

11. B · It can bypass facts and emotional truth
A cheerful replacement story can invalidate the client and remain as rigid as the original.

12. C · Restore awareness, choice, and values-aligned action
The coach helps widen information and return responsible choice to the client.
