LESSON 11 · ANSWER KEY

1. B · How the client will participate
A boundary governs the client's own access, action, and participation.

2. B · If a non-emergency call comes after nine, I will answer tomorrow
It states an observable condition and self-owned response.

3. B · Capacity
Capacity establishes what is realistically available before a limit is designed.

4. B · It expands acceptable options
A valued need may be protected through more than one workable strategy.

5. B · A feeling to examine alongside evidence and values
Intensity alone does not determine whether the boundary is responsible.

6. B · The client knowingly broke a promise and caused preventable harm
Healthy guilt may signal a real value violation and need for repair.

7. B · Lead with the decision and one useful reason
Warm, brief, direct language reduces unnecessary defense and negotiation.

8. B · Acknowledging and restating the same limit
A respectful repeat maintains clarity without escalating.

9. B · Evaluate evidence while allowing both realities
Empathy and a responsible boundary can coexist.

10. B · Proportionate, calm, and within the client's control
Consistency teaches the boundary while preserving dignity.

11. A · Repair the tone while keeping the limit
Accountability for delivery does not erase a legitimate boundary.

12. C · Threats and stalking after a limit
Threats and stalking are safety concerns, not routine boundary discomfort.
