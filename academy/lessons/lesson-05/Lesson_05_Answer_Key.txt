LESSON 5 · ANSWER KEY

1. B · Relevant honesty under uncertainty
Useful vulnerability is chosen, relevant, purposeful, and cannot guarantee the response.

2. B · Volume can ignore purpose, trust, relevance, and consequence
More disclosure is not inherently wiser or more values-aligned.

3. B · A strategy attempting to create safety or control
Function-based curiosity lowers shame and reveals choice.

4. B · She changed the subject when sadness appeared
The statement describes behavior without imposing identity or diagnosis.

5. A · Cue, fear, strategy, relief, cost
The map follows what happened, feared outcome, protection, immediate benefit, and later price.

6. B · Repeated behavior such as reliability, boundaries, honesty, and repair
Behavior over time provides better trust evidence than status or intensity.

7. B · Defining topic, audience, timing, and follow-through
Intentional conditions can make honest action safer and clearer.

8. B · Respect the boundary and choose another useful focus
Consent and client ownership remain essential even during emotionally meaningful work.

9. B · Use graduated, client-owned practice within coaching scope
Proportionate steps build flexible behavior without coercion or overwhelm.

10. B · The client acts in line with values and learns from the result
The client can control participation, not another person's response.

11. B · Prioritize safety and appropriate specialized resources
Ordinary courage practice must not increase danger.

12. B · Increase awareness, discernment, flexible choice, and responsible action
The client gains more options while retaining privacy, boundaries, and agency.
