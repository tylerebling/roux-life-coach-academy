REALISTIC COACHING SCENARIO

Tasha asks for coachingâ€”but her needs have moved beyond ordinary coaching

Tasha, age 34, initially seeks help with boundaries after a breakup. Over several sessions she reports escalating panic, missing work, not eating regularly, monitoring her former partner, and saying life does not feel worth living. Her coach recognizes the change in functioning, asks direct safety questions, follows the documented safety procedure, connects Tasha with qualified care, and does not use attachment language as a diagnosis.

Listen forPresenting goal, functioning, severity, duration, safety, self-harm or harm intent, substance use, abuse, medical concerns, eating and sleep changes, supports, location, emergency contacts, scope, consent, documentation, consultation, and referral follow-through.
Do notDiagnose an attachment disorder, treat trauma or panic, promise confidentiality without limits, provide crisis care beyond competence, contact others without authority except as legally or ethically required, abandon the client, or continue ordinary coaching when unsafe.
